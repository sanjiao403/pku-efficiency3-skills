## 1 Background

Cytosolic DNA sensing converges on the adaptor STING, which must move from the
endoplasmic reticulum to the Golgi to recruit TBK1. How this trafficking step is
gated is unresolved. Here we asked whether S-palmitoylation of STING controls its
exit from the ER and the downstream interferon response.

## 2 Results

We found that the palmitoyl transferase DHHC3 palmitoylates STING at cysteine 88.
Palmitoylated STING is retained in the ER and fails to reach the Golgi. When DHHC3
was depleted, STING trafficking to the Golgi increased 2.4 fold and TBK1
phosphorylation rose 3.1 fold. Activated TBK1 phosphorylates IRF3, and
phosphorylated IRF3 drives IFNB1 transcription. The small molecule 2-bromopalmitate
blocks DHHC3 and phenocopies DHHC3 depletion.

## 3 Quantification

In primary macrophages, IFNB1 mRNA induction at 6 h was 18.5 fold in wild type cells
and 41.2 fold in DHHC3 knockdown cells. STING Golgi colocalisation was 22 percent in
wild type and 61 percent after DHHC3 knockdown. Mean ER residence time of STING
dropped from 46 min to 17 min.

## 4 Conclusion

DHHC3-dependent palmitoylation holds STING in the ER and sets the threshold of the
interferon response. Blocking this modification releases the brake on STING
trafficking and amplifies antiviral signalling, which suggests DHHC3 as a
pharmacological entry point for adjuvants.
